TEPHRA: a volcanic-ash decision desk

When the official ash reports for a volcano disagree, TEPHRA shows how, proposes the cautious area, which is never smaller than the official one, finds the flights heading into it, and drafts the advisory a dispatcher approves.

The problem it works on

The operator must resolve those conflicts (ICAO Doc 9974). TEPHRA drafts that reconciliation for the dispatcher to check.

How it is built

Public feeds (SIGMET, ash advisories, observatory notices, winds, aircraft positions) → code labels every disagreement → TEPHRA Assistant's Coordinator orders the work → the Plume reader (Gemini) grades the evidence and proposes the area → code keeps it at the official floor and finds the flights and a route around it → the Flight planner and Drafter write a draft → a person approves on the Desk → every step is in the hash-chained record.

The public demo runs on Google Cloud: Cloud Run (Singapore), Cloud Scheduler, Firestore, Secret Manager, Artifact Registry; the Gemini API and the Agent Development Kit.

Try it in three minutes

  1. Open the Wall: the amber dashed line is TEPHRA's estimate and the pink area is the official one. Press F to fit the volcano.
  2. Open Details › Where the sources disagree, then TEPHRA's estimate › How the Plume reader read it.
  3. Click an amber plane: its minutes to entry, its draft advisory and its suggested route.
  4. Press A, or open TEPHRA Assistant at work: the Coordinator handing out the day's work.
  5. Open the Desk: decide on a draft (hold to approve), then open its record line.
  6. Details › Track record: how TEPHRA's area scored against the official areas issued later. The operator policy lists every margin it applied, with its reason.

What TEPHRA is and is not

Decision support only. Ring of Fire Air is a fictional airline; its flight numbers stand in for real aircraft. Official reports are binding; TEPHRA's estimate can widen them, never shrink them. Nothing is sent to an aircraft or to any real system. REPLAY, when shown, means recorded or reconstructed real feeds. TEPHRA does not detect ash, is not a forecast, a warning or a dispatch release, and is not air traffic control.
